Privacy

How Family Planner looks after your family's information. Last updated 4 October 2026.

The short version

  • Your family's data is used only to run the app for you. It is never sold, shared for advertising or used to track you.
  • Without an account, everything stays on your own device.
  • With an account, it's also kept online in the UK (London), encrypted, so your devices and household can share it.
  • You can download all of it, or delete your account and its data, at any time from Settings.

Who is responsible

Family Planner is a small app run by Nathan Schofield for a few families. If you have a question or want something changed or deleted, ask the person who invited you to the app, or contact Nathan directly.

What the app keeps

  • Your family: children's first names, birth dates, clothing and shoe sizes, and any diet needs you add.
  • Your home: food in the cupboards and freezer, leftovers, recipes, meal plans, what each of you thought of dinners, foods the children won't have in their lunchboxes, clothes, shopping lists, spending you record and your budget, chores, points and pocket money.
  • Your plans: calendar events such as school and club days, what to take, trips away and their packing lists.
  • Your settings: your town (for the weather), your look and layout choices, and your AI settings.
  • Your account, if you make one: your email address and a password (stored securely by Amazon; nobody can read it).

Children don't have accounts. Only the grown-ups who sign in enter information about them.

Where it's kept

  • On your device, always. Uninstalling the app or clearing the browser's data removes it.
  • Online, if you sign in: in Amazon Web Services in London. Logins use Amazon Cognito, and your household's lists are stored as one encrypted file. Everyone in your household can see that household's lists. Bills you mark as personal are the exception: they're kept in a separate file under your own login, never in the household's, so only you can see them on your devices. The last few saved copies are kept so a mistake can be undone.

When the app talks to other services

  • Weather: your town name is sent to Open-Meteo to find the forecast for outfit ideas, and a trip's destination and dates when you let the app work out its weather.
  • Barcodes: a scanned barcode number is looked up on Open Food Facts. Nothing else is sent.
  • Recipe links: if you add a recipe from a link, the recipe page you paste is fetched so the recipe can be read from it. That website sees the request, as it would if you visited it, but nothing about your family is sent. If you paste the recipe's text instead, nothing is fetched.
  • Shopping links: tapping a supermarket link opens that shop's website with the item's name.
  • Notifications, only if you turn them on (Settings > Notifications, in the version added to your Home Screen): your phone's upcoming reminders are kept with your account, and each one is sent when it's due through Apple's push service (or your browser maker's, on other devices). A notification only contains the reminder's text, like "Milk goes off tomorrow", and is encrypted so only your phone can read it.
  • Siri, only if you set it up (Settings > Siri): the Shortcut on your iPhone sends what you said to the app's server, with a key that lets it add to your household's shopping list, tick off chores and record spending. A sentence it can't do there waits with your household's lists until your own app picks it up. Only a scrambled copy of the key is kept. You can turn the key off in Settings at any time.
  • Feedback: if you use Send feedback in Settings, your message and email address are emailed to Nathan, using Amazon's email service.
  • Fonts: the app's lettering is loaded from Google Fonts, which sees your device's internet address.
  • AI, only if you turn it on: Apple's on-device AI keeps everything on your iPhone. If you choose an AI service (such as Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or OpenRouter) with your own key, the details needed for each suggestion are sent to that service. This can include what's in your cupboards, your children's first names, ages and sizes, and photos you choose to scan. That service's own privacy policy then applies. Your AI key stays on your device and is never uploaded.
  • Ask (the chat): with an AI on, each message you send goes to that AI service with a short summary of your household: first names, ages and sizes, what's on your lists and in the cupboards, chores, the Diary for the next two weeks, trips and this month's spending. Emails, passwords, invite codes and your AI key are never sent. Without an AI, the chat runs on your device and nothing is sent anywhere. Your conversation is kept only on your device, for 7 days or until you tap Clear, and isn't shared with your household.

How long it's kept

Until you delete it. Invite codes expire after 7 days or once used. When you delete your account, your login, your online lists, your personal bills, your notification settings, your Siri key and all the older saved copies are deleted straight away. If you share a household, the others keep its lists, but your email is taken off everything.

Your choices and rights

  • See or take your data: Settings > Backup downloads everything as a file.
  • Correct it: edit anything in the app.
  • Delete it: Settings > Account > Delete my account. Without an account, clearing the app's data on your device removes it.
  • Leave a household at any time from Settings > Account.
  • If you're unhappy with how your data is handled, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Cookies

The app uses no cookies and no tracking or analytics. It saves your settings on your own device so it remembers them.
